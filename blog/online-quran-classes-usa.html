Online Quran Classes in the USA — How It Works

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

For many Muslim families in the USA, finding a good Quran teacher nearby is not easy. A local mosque may offer weekend classes, but the times may not suit your family, the groups may be large, and a qualified female teacher for your daughter or for yourself may not be available at all. That is why so many families in the USA now learn the Quran online, one-to-one, from home.

This guide explains how online Quran classes work for students in the USA — the courses, the class times across American time zones, the fees, and how to start with free trial classes before you decide anything.

Why online Quran classes work well for families in the USA

The USA is a big country, and Muslim families live in every state — in large cities with a mosque on nearly every corner, and in small towns where the nearest Islamic centre is an hour's drive away. Online classes remove the distance completely. Your child learns from your living room, at a time you choose, with a tutor who gives them their full attention for the whole lesson.

Online learning also fits American family life. School, homework, sports and weekend commitments fill the week, and driving to a class after a long day is one more thing to organise. A 30-minute lesson at home, after school or in the evening, is far easier to keep up week after week — and regularity is what makes a child progress in reading the Quran.

For sisters, online classes solve a problem that many families know well: finding a qualified female teacher. At Hafiza Online Academy, certified female tutors teach kids and sisters, so a girl can learn comfortably with a female teacher, and a sister can learn without leaving home. Just ask when you book.

How online Quran classes work

Every class at Hafiza Online Academy is live and one-to-one: one student with one tutor, on a video call. There are no recorded lessons and no group classes where a child can sit quietly and be overlooked. Here is the process, step by step.

Step 1: Book 3 free trial classes

Fill in the free trial form or email us at hafizaonline.it@gmail.com. Tell us the student's age and level, whether you would like a male or female tutor, and what times suit your family in your local time zone. We arrange 3 free classes — no card required and no obligation to continue.

Step 2: Meet your tutor and check the level

In the first class, the tutor checks what the student already knows. Some children know a few Arabic letters; some know none at all; some can read the Quran but want to improve their Tajweed. The tutor starts from exactly the right place, so no time is wasted.

Step 3: Regular live lessons

Lessons are 30 minutes long — 45 minutes for Hifz — on Zoom, Skype or WhatsApp, whichever your family prefers. The tutor shares the lesson on screen, the student reads aloud, and the tutor corrects pronunciation straight away. Between lessons, a few minutes of daily practice keeps the learning fresh.

Step 4: Progress and feedback for parents

Parents receive regular updates on what their child is learning and what to practise. If a lesson time stops working — a new school term, a change of routine — the time can be changed. And if the tutor is not the right fit, you can ask for a different one.

Class times across US time zones

The USA spans four main time zones — Eastern, Central, Mountain and Pacific — so a class time that works in New York is three hours earlier in California. That is why we never set one fixed time for everyone. When you book, you tell us your time zone and the times that suit your family, and we schedule around your local time.

Most families in the USA choose after-school or evening classes — typically between about 4 pm and 8 pm local time on weekdays, with morning or afternoon slots on weekends. Because our tutors teach students in many countries, they are used to working across time zones and to keeping early-morning or late-evening hours so that a lesson lands at a sensible time for your child.

Daylight saving changes in spring and autumn can shift the difference between your time and your tutor's time by an hour. If that happens, just let us know and we will adjust the lesson time so it stays at the same hour for your family.

Which course should your child start with?

We teach four courses, and the tutor will place your child in the right one after the first trial class:

  • Noorani Qaida — for complete beginners. Children from around 4 or 5 years old learn the Arabic alphabet, the vowel marks, and how to join letters into words.
  • Nazra — Quran Reading — for students who have finished the Qaida. They read the Quran itself, building fluency page by page.
  • Tajweed — for students who can read but want to read correctly, with the proper rules of pronunciation.
  • Hifz — Memorization — for students who want to memorise the Quran, with longer 45-minute lessons and a structured revision plan.

Many families in the USA start a young child on Noorani Qaida, while an older child who learned some Quran at a weekend school starts with Nazra or Tajweed. Adults and sisters are welcome at every level, including complete beginners.

Two days a week costs $25 a month, three days a week costs $35 a month, and five days a week costs $55 a month. Hifz, with its longer 45-minute lessons, is $65 a month for five days a week.

Three days a week at $35 is the most popular choice: it is regular enough for steady progress without taking over the family's evenings. A family discount is available on request if two or more children from the same family enrol. See the full details on our fees page.

"A child who reads a little every week, with a tutor who listens to every letter, will read the Quran better than a child who is rushed through it in a crowded class."

Tips for parents in the USA

  • Pick a time your child can keep. Right after school, or after dinner and homework — whichever is calmer in your home. A regular slot is easier to keep than a different time every week.
  • Sit nearby for young children. You do not need to know Arabic. Just being in the room keeps a young child focused and keeps the lesson safe.
  • Keep practice short and daily. Five minutes of reading the current page each day matters more than one long session at the weekend.
  • Talk to the tutor. If progress feels slow, or the time no longer works, say so. The schedule and the pace can be adjusted.
  • Use the trial classes properly. Three free classes are enough to see whether your child is comfortable with the tutor and the routine before you pay anything.
