Free Trial Quran Classes — What to Check Before You Pay

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

Almost every online Quran academy offers a free trial. That is a good thing — it means you can try before you pay. But not all free trials are equal. Some are real lessons with a real teacher. Others are a short sales call dressed up as a class, or a single rushed demo that tells you nothing about what the paid lessons will be like.

This guide explains what a good free trial should include, what to watch for in the trial lessons, and which questions to ask before you pay anything. Use it to judge any academy — including ours.

What a good free trial should include

A free trial is only useful if it shows you the real experience. Before you book, check that the trial offers these things:

Real lessons, not a demo

The trial should be actual teaching, not a presentation about the academy. Your child — or you — should read, be corrected, and learn something in the lesson. If the whole trial is spent talking about fees and schedules, it is not a trial lesson.

More than one class

One lesson is not enough to judge a teacher. The first lesson is always a little awkward: new tutor, new format, new routine. By the second or third lesson, you can see how the tutor teaches, how your child responds, and whether the pace is right. A trial of 3 classes is a fair test. Hafiza Online Academy offers 3 free trial classes for exactly this reason.

One-to-one teaching

The trial lesson should be one student with one tutor — the same format as the paid lessons. A group demo tells you nothing about how your child will be taught one-to-one.

No card required

A genuine free trial does not ask for your card details. If an academy asks for payment details "just to hold the trial", be cautious. A trial that costs nothing should ask for nothing.

Your choice of platform and time

The trial should run on the platform you prefer — Zoom, Skype or WhatsApp — and at a time that suits your family. If the academy only offers one platform or one fixed time, that is a sign the paid lessons may be inflexible too.

A level check

In the first lesson, the tutor should check what the student already knows and start from the right place. A tutor who opens the Qaida at page one without asking anything has not bothered to find out where your child is.

What to watch for in the trial lessons

During the three trial classes, pay attention to these things. They matter far more than a polished website.

Does the tutor correct pronunciation?

This is the single most important thing. A good Quran tutor listens carefully and corrects mistakes immediately and gently. If the tutor lets misread letters pass without comment, your child will practise the mistake until it becomes a habit. In the trial, notice whether the tutor stops, corrects, and asks the student to repeat.

Does your child feel comfortable?

Watch your child's face at the end of the lesson, not just during it. A child who is relaxed, smiling, or keen to show you what they learned is a child who will keep coming back. A child who is upset, bored or frightened is telling you the match is wrong — even if the tutor is technically good.

Is the pace right?

A good tutor adjusts the pace to the student. If the lesson races ahead, the student will be lost. If it crawls, the student will be bored. After two or three lessons, the pace should feel steady: some new material, some revision, and time to get things right.

Does the tutor explain what to practise?

After each lesson, you should know what the student should practise before the next one — which page, which letters, for how long. If you are left guessing, the academy may not be organised enough to support you after you start paying.

Can you reach the academy easily?

Try contacting them with a question during the trial — by email or through the trial form — and see how quickly and clearly they reply. The way an academy treats you before you pay is usually the way it will treat you after.

Questions to ask before you pay

Before you commit to any academy, get clear answers to these questions:

  • What are the fees, exactly? Ask for the monthly price for the number of days you want. At Hafiza Online Academy the fees are published on the fees page: 2 days $25, 3 days $35, 5 days $55, and Hifz 5 days $65 per month, in USD. There is no registration fee.
  • Can I cancel anytime? You should be able to stop without a penalty or a notice period that locks you in. We allow cancellation anytime.
  • Can I change the tutor? Sometimes the match is not right. You should be able to ask for a different tutor, including a female tutor, without a fuss.
  • Can I change the schedule? Life changes — school times shift, families travel. A good academy lets you change lesson days and times.
  • What happens if we miss a lesson? Ask about the policy for missed or rescheduled classes before you pay, not after.
  • Is there a family discount? If two or more children in your family will take classes, ask. A family discount is available on request at our academy.
  • How will I know my child is progressing? Parents should receive regular updates on what their child is learning. Ask how that works.

If an academy cannot answer these questions clearly, or the answers keep changing, take that as a warning sign.

Red flags to watch for

A few warning signs should make you pause:

  • Pressure to pay immediately. "This price is only available today" is a sales tactic, not a reason to sign up. A good academy lets you decide in your own time.
  • Card details for a "free" trial. A free trial should not need your card.
  • Hidden fees. Registration fees, material fees, or charges that only appear after you sign up. Ask for the full cost in writing.
  • No clear tutor information. You should know whether your tutor is certified and whether you can request a male or female tutor.
  • No way to contact them. A real email address and a real reply matter. Our contact email is hafizaonline.it@gmail.com.
