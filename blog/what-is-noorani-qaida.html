What Is Noorani Qaida and How to Learn It Online

November 2026 · Hafiz Online Academy

Almost every child who learns to read the Quran starts in the same place: Noorani Qaida. It is a short beginner's primer that teaches the Arabic alphabet, the sounds of each letter, and the rules for joining letters into words. Generations of Muslim families across South Asia and around the world have used it as the first step before opening the Quran itself.

If you are a parent wondering where your child's Quran journey begins, or an adult who never learned to read Arabic, this guide explains what Noorani Qaida is, what it covers, and how learning it online works in practice.

What exactly is Noorani Qaida?

Noorani Qaida is a small booklet — usually around 20 to 30 pages — that introduces reading Arabic step by step. It does not teach the meaning of the Quran, and it is not a translation. Its single job is to teach you to recognise and pronounce Arabic letters correctly, and then to read words fluently.

The Qaida builds in a careful order. Each lesson adds one new idea on top of what came before, so a complete beginner is never asked to do something they have not been prepared for. A tutor works through it page by page, listening to the student read aloud and correcting mistakes on the spot.

What does Noorani Qaida teach?

The booklet is organised into clear stages. Here is what a student works through, in order:

1. The Arabic alphabet

First come the 29 letters of the Arabic alphabet, from Alif to Yaa. The student learns each letter's name, its shape, and its sound. Some letters sound similar to English speakers — Ba, Ta, Meem — while others, like 'Ain, Ha and Qaaf, have no English equivalent and need careful listening and practice.

2. Harakat — the short vowel marks

Next come the harakat: Fatha, Kasra and Damma. These small marks above or below a letter tell you which short vowel sound to make. Learning them is the moment reading starts to click, because letters stop being isolated sounds and become syllables.

3. Joining letters into words

Arabic letters change shape depending on where they sit in a word — at the start, in the middle, or at the end. The Qaida teaches these joined forms gradually, so the student moves from reading single letters to reading whole words.

4. Tanween, Sukoon and Shaddah

Later lessons add Tanween (the "n" sound at the end of a word), Sukoon (a letter with no vowel, read plainly), and Shaddah (a doubled letter, read with emphasis). These are the marks a student sees constantly in the Quran, so learning them in the Qaida means there are no surprises later.

5. Madd and basic reading rules

The final lessons introduce Madd — stretching a sound — and the basic rules for stopping and starting. By the end of the Qaida, a student can read simple Arabic words and short sentences with reasonable fluency, and is ready to begin Nazra, reading the Quran itself.

Who should learn Noorani Qaida?

Noorani Qaida is for anyone who cannot yet read Arabic script. That includes:

  • Children — most children can start once they can sit and focus for a short lesson, often from around 4 or 5 years old.
  • Adult beginners — many adults grew up without learning to read Arabic, or learned it as children and forgot. The Qaida works just as well for them.
  • New Muslims — anyone coming to the Quran for the first time starts here.
  • Students returning after a break — even if you read the Quran years ago, a quick pass through the Qaida rebuilds the foundations.

You do not need to know any Arabic beforehand, and you do not need to speak Arabic. The Qaida teaches reading, not conversation.

How does learning Noorani Qaida online work?

Online Qaida lessons follow the same page-by-page method as a traditional class, but one-to-one on a video call. Here is how it works at Hafiz Online Academy:

Step 2: Level check

In the first class, the tutor checks what the student already knows. Some children know a few letters; some know none at all. The tutor starts from exactly the right page, so no time is wasted.

Step 3: Live one-to-one lessons

Each lesson is one student with one tutor, live on video. The tutor shares the Qaida page on screen, the student reads aloud, and the tutor corrects pronunciation immediately. This is the great advantage of one-to-one teaching: in a group, a child can hide a mispronounced letter for weeks. One-to-one, every mistake is heard and fixed.

Step 4: Short daily practice

Between lessons, the student practises the current page for a few minutes a day. For young children, parents sitting nearby and listening — even without knowing Arabic themselves — makes a real difference, because the child knows someone is paying attention.

Step 5: Moving on to Nazra

When the student finishes the Qaida and can read its final pages fluently, the tutor moves them on to Nazra — reading the Quran itself, starting from the first pages of the Mushaf.

How long does Noorani Qaida take?

It depends on the student's age, how often lessons happen, and how much they practise between lessons. As a general guide, a child taking classes 3 days a week and practising a little each day often finishes the Qaida in a few months. Adults who already know the alphabet from another language, or who practise more, can move faster. Younger children, or students taking 2 classes a week, may take a little longer — and that is completely fine.

There is no prize for rushing. A student who reads the Qaida slowly but correctly will read the Quran far better than one who was hurried through it with shaky pronunciation. A good tutor will repeat a lesson rather than push a student forward before they are ready.

"The Qaida is short, but it carries the whole journey. Every correct letter your child learns here is a letter they will read correctly in the Quran for the rest of their life."

Tips for parents

  • Keep lessons regular. Three short lessons a week beat one long lesson. Little and often is how children learn sounds.
  • Sit nearby for young children. You do not need to know Arabic — just being present keeps a young child focused, and it keeps the lesson safe.
  • Praise effort, not speed. A child who is praised for trying will keep trying. Comparing them to a sibling or cousin only slows them down.
  • Ask the tutor for a progress update. At Hafiz Online Academy, parents receive regular updates on what their child is learning and what to practise.
  • Choose a female tutor if that suits your family. We have certified female tutors for kids and sisters, at no extra cost — just ask when you book.
