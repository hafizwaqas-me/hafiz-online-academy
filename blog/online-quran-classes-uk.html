Online Quran Classes in the UK — How It Works

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

Muslim families in the UK have no shortage of love for the Quran — but finding the right teacher is not always simple. A local mosque or madrasah may run evening classes, yet the groups are often large, the times may clash with school and homework, and a qualified female teacher for your daughter or for yourself may not be available. That is why many families across the UK now choose live one-to-one Quran classes online, from home.

This guide explains how online Quran classes work for students in the UK — the courses, the class times on UK time, the fees, and how to start with free trial classes before you decide anything.

Why online Quran classes work well for families in the UK

The UK has large Muslim communities in cities such as London, Birmingham, Manchester and Bradford, and smaller communities spread across the rest of the country. Wherever you live, an online class removes the journey: no driving in the rain to the mosque after a long school day, no waiting around while one child finishes and another starts. Your child learns from your living room, at a time you choose.

Online classes also fit the British school week. Children come home tired, with homework to do, and an evening trip to a class is one more thing to organise. A 30-minute lesson at home, after school or in the early evening, is far easier to keep up week after week — and regularity is what makes a child progress in reading the Quran.

For sisters, online classes solve a problem many families know well: finding a qualified female teacher. At Hafiza Online Academy, certified female tutors teach kids and sisters, so a girl can learn comfortably with a female teacher, and a sister can learn without leaving home. Just ask when you book.

How online Quran classes work

Every class at Hafiza Online Academy is live and one-to-one: one student with one tutor, on a video call. There are no recorded lessons and no group classes where a child can sit quietly and be overlooked. Here is the process, step by step.

Step 1: Book 3 free trial classes

Fill in the free trial form or email us at hafizaonline.it@gmail.com. Tell us the student's age and level, whether you would like a male or female tutor, and what times suit your family on UK time. We arrange 3 free classes — no card required and no obligation to continue.

Step 2: Meet your tutor and check the level

In the first class, the tutor checks what the student already knows. Some children know a few Arabic letters; some know none at all; some can read the Quran but want to improve their Tajweed. The tutor starts from exactly the right place, so no time is wasted.

Step 3: Regular live lessons

Lessons are 30 minutes long — 45 minutes for Hifz — on Zoom, Skype or WhatsApp, whichever your family prefers. The tutor shares the lesson on screen, the student reads aloud, and the tutor corrects pronunciation straight away. Between lessons, a few minutes of daily practice keeps the learning fresh.

Step 4: Progress and feedback for parents

Parents receive regular updates on what their child is learning and what to practise. If a lesson time stops working — a new school term, a change of routine — the time can be changed. And if the tutor is not the right fit, you can ask for a different one.

Class times on UK time

The UK runs on Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) in winter and British Summer Time (BST) in summer, one hour ahead. When you book, you tell us the times that suit your family in UK time, and we schedule around them — you never need to work out the time difference yourself.

Most families in the UK choose after-school or early-evening classes — typically between about 4 pm and 8 pm on weekdays, with morning or afternoon slots on weekends. That fits well around school, homework and family time, and it avoids the late evenings that make young children too tired to learn.

The clocks change in spring and autumn, which can shift the difference between UK time and your tutor's time by an hour. If that happens, just let us know and we will adjust the lesson time so it stays at the same hour for your family.

Which course should your child start with?

We teach four courses, and the tutor will place your child in the right one after the first trial class:

  • Noorani Qaida — for complete beginners. Children from around 4 or 5 years old learn the Arabic alphabet, the vowel marks, and how to join letters into words.
  • Nazra — Quran Reading — for students who have finished the Qaida. They read the Quran itself, building fluency page by page.
  • Tajweed — for students who can read but want to read correctly, with the proper rules of pronunciation.
  • Hifz — Memorization — for students who want to memorise the Quran, with longer 45-minute lessons and a structured revision plan.

Many families in the UK start a young child on Noorani Qaida, while an older child who learned some Quran at a madrasah starts with Nazra or Tajweed. Adults and sisters are welcome at every level, including complete beginners.

"A child who reads a little every week, with a tutor who listens to every letter, will read the Quran better than a child who is rushed through it in a crowded class."

Tips for parents in the UK

  • Pick a time your child can keep. Right after school, or after dinner and homework — whichever is calmer in your home. A regular slot is easier to keep than a different time every week.
  • Sit nearby for young children. You do not need to know Arabic. Just being in the room keeps a young child focused and keeps the lesson safe.
  • Keep practice short and daily. Five minutes of reading the current page each day matters more than one long session at the weekend.
  • Talk to the tutor. If progress feels slow, or the time no longer works, say so. The schedule and the pace can be adjusted.
  • Use the trial classes properly. Three free classes are enough to see whether your child is comfortable with the tutor and the routine before you pay anything.
