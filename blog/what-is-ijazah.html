What Is Ijazah and How to Get Ijazah Certification Online

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

Ijazah is one of the oldest traditions in Islamic learning, and one of the least understood outside it. In simple terms, an Ijazah is a licence — a certificate given by a qualified teacher to a student, authorising that student to teach or transmit what they have learned. In the context of the Quran, it means that a certified teacher has listened to the student recite the entire Quran, found their recitation correct according to the rules of Tajweed, and granted them permission to teach it to others.

This guide explains what Ijazah is, how the chain of transmission works, what it takes to earn one, and how the path can begin online.

What does Ijazah mean?

The word Ijazah comes from an Arabic root meaning to permit or authorise. When a teacher grants Ijazah, they are saying: I have heard this student recite, their recitation is correct, and I permit them to teach what they have learned from me.

It is not a degree in the modern sense, and it is not awarded for passing a written exam. It is a personal authorisation, given face to face — or, in online learning, voice to voice — by a teacher who holds Ijazah themselves. That personal link is the whole point.

The chain of transmission (Sanad)

Every Ijazah comes with a Sanad — a chain of teachers stretching back through the generations. The student's teacher received Ijazah from their teacher, who received it from theirs, and so on, in an unbroken chain that goes back to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), who received the Quran from the Angel Jibreel.

When you receive Ijazah, your name is added to that chain. The written certificate records the chain, so that anyone can see who taught you and who taught them. This is why Ijazah is so valued: it is not just a statement that you can recite well, but a documented link to a tradition of transmission that has continued for over fourteen centuries.

Different chains exist for different readings (Qira'at) of the Quran. The most common is the reading of Hafs from 'Asim, which is the reading used in most of the Muslim world, including Pakistan, the Middle East and the West. A student usually earns Ijazah in one reading first, and may later earn Ijazah in others.

What kinds of Ijazah are there?

Ijazah in recitation (Tilawah)

The most common form. The student recites the entire Quran to a certified teacher, from memory or from the Mushaf depending on the programme, with correct Tajweed throughout. The teacher listens to every verse and corrects any mistake. When the whole Quran has been recited to the teacher's satisfaction, Ijazah is granted.

Ijazah in memorization (Hifz)

For students who have memorized the Quran. The student recites the entire Quran from memory to the teacher, usually over many sessions, with correct Tajweed and without significant errors. This is a demanding test: the teacher may ask the student to begin from any verse, to check that the memorization is firm and not just a habit of reading in order.

Ijazah in Tajweed

Some programmes grant Ijazah for mastery of the science of Tajweed itself — the rules, their evidence and their application. This is usually combined with recitation of the whole Quran.

What does it take to earn Ijazah?

Earning Ijazah is a serious undertaking. The requirements are demanding, and they should be — the teacher is putting their own name and chain behind the student's recitation. In general, a student needs:

  • Fluent reading of the Quran. The student must already read the Quran fluently and correctly. Ijazah is not a beginner's course; it is the stage after Tajweed has been learned properly.
  • Strong Tajweed. Every rule must be applied consistently — Makharij, Noon Sakinah, Meem Sakinah, Madd, Qalqalah, Waqf and the rest. Occasional slips are corrected during the process; persistent errors are not accepted.
  • Recitation of the whole Quran to the teacher. The core of the process is reading the entire Quran aloud to a certified teacher, who listens to every word. For a memorization Ijazah, the whole Quran is recited from memory.
  • Time and patience. The process takes as long as it takes. A student may need months or longer to work through the whole Quran with the teacher, depending on how often lessons happen and how much revision is needed. There is no shortcut, and no teacher who values their chain will promise Ijazah by a fixed date.

It is worth saying plainly: Ijazah is not for everyone, and it is not a requirement for a Muslim. Most people who read the Quran well will never seek Ijazah, and that is completely fine. It is a path for those who want to teach, or who want the deep discipline of perfecting their recitation under a certified teacher.

Can you get Ijazah online?

Yes. Online Ijazah programmes have become common, and they work because the heart of Ijazah is listening: a teacher listening to a student recite, live, and correcting them. That can happen over a video call just as it can happen in a room together. The student recites, the teacher listens and corrects, and the sessions continue until the whole Quran has been recited.

What matters is the teacher's own qualification. An Ijazah is only as sound as the chain behind it, so a genuine programme will be open about who the teacher is, who granted them Ijazah, and which reading the Ijazah covers. Any programme that promises Ijazah in a set number of weeks, without listening to your recitation first, should be treated with caution.

"Ijazah is not a certificate you buy or a course you finish. It is a teacher saying: I have heard you recite the whole Quran, and I trust you to teach it to others. That trust is earned, one verse at a time."

How to start the Ijazah path

If you are interested in Ijazah, the first step is an honest assessment of where your recitation stands. A certified tutor can listen to you read, tell you how close you are to the standard required, and map out what needs work — usually Tajweed first, then the long process of reciting the whole Quran to the teacher.

For most students, the practical path looks like this: first, complete Noorani Qaida and Nazra, then study Tajweed until the rules are applied consistently, and then begin the Ijazah recitation itself. Students who have already memorized the Quran may work towards a Hifz Ijazah instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Ijazah and a Hifz certificate?

A Hifz certificate usually says that a student has memorized the Quran. An Ijazah says more: that a certified teacher has listened to the student's recitation of the whole Quran, found it correct according to Tajweed, and authorised them to teach it — and it comes with a chain of transmission (Sanad) back through generations of teachers.

Do I need to be a Hafiz to get Ijazah?

No. Ijazah in recitation (Tilawah) is earned by reciting the whole Quran to a teacher, reading from the Mushaf. A separate Ijazah exists for memorization (Hifz), where the whole Quran is recited from memory. Both are genuine forms of Ijazah.

How long does it take to earn Ijazah?

There is no fixed time. It depends on the student's current level, how often lessons happen, and how much revision is needed. The process takes as long as it takes to recite the whole Quran correctly to the teacher's satisfaction, and no genuine programme will promise a date in advance.
