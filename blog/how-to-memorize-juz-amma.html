How to Memorize Juz Amma (Short Surahs) First

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

Juz Amma — the 30th and final Juz of the Quran — is where most Hifz journeys begin. It contains 37 short surahs, from Surah An-Naba to Surah An-Nas, and many of them are surahs you already know by heart, or partly know, from prayer. For a child or an adult starting to memorize, that familiarity is a gift: the first steps feel achievable, and each completed surah is a visible win.

This guide explains why Juz Amma is the usual starting point, how to memorize it step by step, and how to revise it so that it stays with you long after you move on to longer surahs.

Why start with Juz Amma?

There are three good reasons to start here. First, the surahs are short. A child can memorize a whole surah in a day or two, and that sense of completion keeps motivation high. Long surahs, by contrast, can feel endless to a beginner, and it is easy to lose heart before finishing the first one.

Second, you already hear these surahs often. Surah Al-Ikhlas, Surah Al-Falaq, Surah An-Nas and Surah Al-Fatiha (which is in Juz 1, but is recited in every prayer) are part of daily Salah, and the short surahs of Juz Amma are recited in Taraweeh and in daily prayers around the world. Your ear already knows their rhythm, even if your memory does not yet hold every word.

Third, Juz Amma teaches the habits of Hifz on easy material. You learn how to take a small Sabaq, how to repeat it, how to revise it the next day, and how to recite it to a tutor — all while the text itself is short and manageable. Those habits, learned here, carry you through the longer Juz that follow.

What should you know before you start?

You should be able to read the Quran fluently with correct Tajweed before you start memorizing. If you are still working on reading, finish Noorani Qaida and Nazra first — memorizing from a text you cannot read fluently makes every lesson harder, and mistakes learned early are hard to correct later.

You should also use one Mushaf and stick to it. Every printed Mushaf has the same words, but the layout — where a verse starts and ends on the page — differs between editions. Your memory is partly visual: you remember where a verse sits on the page. Changing Mushaf halfway through confuses that visual memory, so choose one copy and use it for all your memorization and revision.

How to memorize Juz Amma, step by step

Step 1: Start from the end

Most students work backwards through Juz Amma, starting with the shortest surahs at the end — Surah An-Nas, Surah Al-Falaq, Surah Al-Ikhlas — and moving towards the longer surahs at the start of the Juz, like Surah An-Naba. Starting with the shortest surahs gives quick wins and builds confidence. Your tutor will set the exact order, but working from the end is the usual pattern.

Step 2: Listen first

Before you try to memorize a surah, listen to it recited correctly several times — by your tutor, or by a reciter you trust. Listen for the Tajweed, the rhythm, and the places where verses join. Trying to memorize a surah you have never heard properly is like trying to sing a song you have never heard: you will guess at the tune and guess wrong.

Step 3: Memorize one verse at a time

Read the first verse aloud, looking at the Mushaf, several times. Then close the Mushaf and try to recite it from memory. If you stumble, look again, repeat it, and try once more. When the first verse is firm, add the second verse, then recite the two together. Keep adding one verse at a time, always reciting from the beginning, until you can recite the whole surah.

Step 4: Recite it to someone

Reciting to your tutor — or to a parent — is essential. When you recite alone, your mind fills in gaps and skips mistakes without you noticing. When you recite to someone who is following in the Mushaf, every missed word and every Tajweed mistake is heard and corrected. In a one-to-one lesson, your tutor listens to every verse, so nothing slips through.

Step 5: Revise it the next day, and the day after

A surah memorized today is not yet learned. Recite it again tomorrow, and the day after, as part of your Sabqi — the revision of recent lessons. After a few days of revision it will start to feel firm, and then it joins your Manzil, the regular revision of everything you have memorized. Our article on Sabaq, Sabqi and Manzil explains this system in full.

How long does Juz Amma take?

It varies a lot. A child taking daily lessons and practising at home might finish Juz Amma in a few months. An adult taking fewer lessons a week will take longer, and that is completely fine. The short surahs at the end go quickly — sometimes one or two a day — while the longer surahs at the start of the Juz, like Surah An-Naba and Surah An-Nazi'at, take several days each.

How to keep Juz Amma strong after you finish it

Finishing Juz Amma is a wonderful milestone — celebrate it. But the work is not over. Juz Amma needs to stay in your Manzil, recited regularly, or it will fade just like any other memorization. The good news is that it is short enough to recite often: the whole Juz takes about twenty to thirty minutes to recite, so it can be covered in a single sitting, or split across a few days.

Many families make Juz Amma part of daily life: reciting a few surahs in Salah, listening to them in the car, or reciting them before bed. The more you use what you have memorized, the stronger it becomes. Memorization that is used in prayer is memorization that lasts.

"Juz Amma is short, but it is not small. It holds the surahs you will recite in prayer for the rest of your life — learn them well, and they will serve you every single day."

Tips for memorizing Juz Amma

  • Use one Mushaf. Stick to the same copy for memorization and revision, so your visual memory stays consistent.
  • Learn the meaning. You do not need to understand Arabic fluently, but knowing the general meaning of a short surah helps you remember its order and its message.
  • Recite in Salah. Use the surahs you have memorized in your prayers. It is the best revision there is, and it gives your memorization a purpose beyond the lesson.
  • Revise little and often. A few minutes of revision every day beats a long session once a week. Short, regular contact keeps the surahs fresh.
  • Do not skip the longer surahs. It is tempting to stay with the short, easy surahs. The longer ones at the start of the Juz are the ones that build your stamina for the rest of the Quran.

Frequently asked questions

How many surahs are in Juz Amma?

Juz Amma, the 30th Juz, contains 37 surahs, from Surah An-Naba (78) to Surah An-Nas (114). Most of them are short, which is why it is the usual starting point for Hifz.

Should I memorize Juz Amma from the start or the end?

Most students work backwards, starting with the shortest surahs at the end — Surah An-Nas, Surah Al-Falaq and Surah Al-Ikhlas — and moving towards the longer surahs at the start of the Juz. Your tutor will set the order that suits you.

Can my child memorize Juz Amma online?

Yes. Juz Amma is ideal for online learning: the surahs are short, the tutor can listen to every verse one-to-one, and the child can recite from home on Zoom, Skype or WhatsApp. Our Hifz course starts many children with Juz Amma.

How do I revise Juz Amma after finishing it?

Keep it in your Manzil — the regular revision of older memorization. Recite a few surahs each day, use them in Salah, and work through the whole Juz regularly so it stays strong while you move on to longer Juz.
