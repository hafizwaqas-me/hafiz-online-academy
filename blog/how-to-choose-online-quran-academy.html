How to Choose the Best Online Quran Academy

November 2026 · Hafiz Online Academy

There are many online Quran academies to choose from — and they all say similar things on their websites. So how do you tell a good one from a poor one? The answer is not the prettiest website or the lowest price. It is a short list of practical checks, and most of them take only a few minutes.

Use this checklist before you enrol your child — or yourself — anywhere.

1. One-to-one or group classes?

This is the first question to ask, and the most important. In a one-to-one class, one student learns with one tutor, live. The tutor hears every letter the student reads and corrects every mistake immediately. In a group class, the tutor's attention is shared, and a child can mispronounce a letter for weeks without anyone noticing.

For Quran learning — where correct pronunciation is the entire point — one-to-one teaching is the standard you should expect. Group classes may be cheaper, but they are a different product. If an academy says "live classes" without saying "one-to-one", ask directly: how many students are in the class?

At Hafiz Online Academy, every class is one-to-one, always. There are no group classes.

2. Are the tutors certified?

A Quran tutor should be qualified to teach the Quran — not just able to read it. Look for tutors who are Hafiz or Hafiza, and Ijazah-certified where they teach Tajweed. Ijazah means the tutor has been authorised to teach by their own teacher, in an unbroken chain of transmission. It is the traditional standard, and it matters.

Also ask whether tutors are background-checked. You are trusting a stranger with your child on a video call — background checks are a basic expectation, not a luxury.

A trustworthy academy will answer these questions plainly. If the answers are vague — "our tutors are all qualified" with no detail — that is a warning sign.

3. Are female tutors available?

Many families prefer a female teacher for their children, and many sisters prefer a female teacher for themselves. A good academy offers certified female tutors without fuss and without extra cost.

Ask the question before you enrol, not after. At Hafiz Online Academy, certified female tutors are available for kids and sisters — just ask when you book, at no extra cost.

4. Is there a real free trial?

Never pay an academy before your child has had real trial lessons with the actual tutor they will learn with. A free trial should mean real lessons — not a recorded video, not a sales call, and not a single five-minute chat.

Our trial is 3 full classes, with no card required. Use those classes to judge the tutor: do they correct mistakes? Does your child enjoy the lesson? Do you get a clear sense of what comes next? Those three questions tell you more than any website can.

5. Are the fees transparent?

The full fee should be published clearly on the website — monthly price, what it includes, and whether there is a registration fee. If the price is hidden behind "contact us", or if a registration fee only appears after you sign up, be cautious.

Our fees are simple: $25 a month for 2 days a week, $35 for 3 days a week (most popular), $55 for 5 days a week, and $65 for the Hifz program. No registration fee, cancel anytime, and a family discount on request. You can see the full details on our fees page.

As a rough guide, other academies commonly charge around $30 to $85 a month for one-to-one classes. Price alone does not tell you much — a cheap academy with unqualified tutors is poor value at any price — but a fee far outside that range, in either direction, is worth asking about.

6. Flexible timing and your platform

Your family lives in one timezone and the tutor in another. A good academy arranges class times around your schedule — before or after school, in the evening, at weekends — not around theirs. Ask whether you can choose your own time slots, and whether you can change them later.

Also check the platform. Lessons should run on a platform you already know and trust — Zoom, Skype or WhatsApp — not on an unfamiliar app you have to install and learn. At Hafiz Online Academy, you choose whichever platform you prefer.

7. Progress updates for parents

You should always know what your child is learning. A good academy keeps parents informed — what page the child is on, what they are doing well, and what to practise. If you have to chase the academy for months to find out whether your child is progressing, something is wrong.

Ask before you enrol: how will I know how my child is doing? The answer should be specific, not a promise to "keep you updated".

8. A clear learning path

A good academy has a path: Noorani Qaida for beginners, then Nazra (fluent reading), then Tajweed, then Hifz for those who want it. The tutor should be able to tell you where your child is on that path, and what comes next. If the academy cannot describe the path, it probably does not have one.

"The best academy is not the one with the biggest claims. It is the one that answers your questions plainly, lets you try before you pay, and keeps you informed once you start."

Red flags to watch for

  • No free trial — or a "trial" that is not a real lesson.
  • Payment demanded before any class — especially by card, with no trial first.
  • Hidden or unclear fees — registration fees, "materials" charges, or prices that only appear after you sign up.
  • Vague tutor qualifications — "all our tutors are qualified" with no mention of Hifz, Ijazah or background checks.
  • Group classes sold as one-to-one — always ask how many students are in the class.
  • Unrealistic promises — full Hifz in a few months, or fluent reading in weeks. Quran learning takes time, and honest academies say so.
  • No way to contact a real person — no email, no phone, no response. You should be able to reach the academy easily. You can reach us at hafizaonline.it@gmail.com.
  • Pressure to pay quickly — "this discount ends today" is a sales tactic, not a teaching method.

Questions to ask before enrolling

Before you commit to any academy, ask these questions — by email, so you have the answers in writing:

  1. Are the classes one-to-one, with one student and one tutor?
  2. What are the tutor's qualifications — Hafiz/Hafiza? Ijazah-certified? Background-checked?
  3. Can I request a female tutor?
  4. How many free trial classes do you offer, and are they real lessons with the actual tutor?
  5. What is the full monthly fee, and is there a registration fee?
  6. Can I cancel anytime? Is there a contract?
  7. What happens if we miss a class — is there a make-up lesson?
  8. What platform are lessons on — Zoom, Skype, WhatsApp?
  9. Can we choose our own class times, in our timezone?
  10. How will I know how my child is progressing?

An academy that answers all ten clearly and willingly is an academy worth trying. One that dodges them is telling you something too.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important thing to check when choosing an academy?

Whether classes are truly one-to-one, and whether the tutors are certified and background-checked. Those two things decide the quality of the teaching more than anything else.

Should I choose the cheapest academy?

Not on price alone. Compare what is included — one-to-one or group, tutor qualifications, lesson length, free trial — and then judge the price. A cheap class that teaches nothing is poor value.

How do I know if the trial went well?

Ask three questions: did the tutor correct mistakes, did your child enjoy the lesson, and do you know what comes next? If the answer is yes to all three, the academy is worth continuing with.

Can I change tutor or plan after enrolling?

At Hafiz Online Academy, yes. If the tutor is not the right match, ask for a change. You can also change plans — for example from 2 days a week to 3 — and you can cancel anytime. Ask any academy the same question before you enrol.
