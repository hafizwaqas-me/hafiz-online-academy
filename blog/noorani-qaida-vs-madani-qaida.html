Noorani Qaida vs Madani Qaida — Which One to Start With?

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

If you are choosing a first book for your child to learn to read the Quran, you will probably come across two names: Noorani Qaida and Madani Qaida. Both are beginner primers. Both teach the Arabic alphabet, the vowel marks, and how to join letters into words. So which one should you start with?

The short answer is that either one can work, and the choice matters far less than the teaching. But the two books do differ in their approach, their history, and where they are most commonly used. This guide explains those differences fairly, so you can make an informed choice for your family.

What is Noorani Qaida?

Noorani Qaida is the most widely used beginner's primer in South Asia and in Muslim communities around the world. It is a short booklet — usually around 20 to 30 pages — that introduces reading Arabic step by step: the 29 letters, the harakat (Fatha, Kasra and Damma), joining letters into words, Tanween, Sukoon, Shaddah, and the basic rules of Madd and stopping.

Its method is gradual. Each lesson adds one new idea on top of what came before, so a complete beginner is never asked to do something they have not been prepared for. Generations of families in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and the diaspora in the USA, UK, Canada and Australia have started with this book, which is why tutors everywhere know it well and why learning materials for it are easy to find.

What is Madani Qaida?

Madani Qaida is another beginner's primer, published by Dawat-e-Islami, a religious organisation based in Pakistan. It covers the same ground as Noorani Qaida — the alphabet, harakat, joining, and the basic reading rules — but with its own layout, exercises and ordering of lessons.

Madani Qaida is used widely in communities connected with Dawat-e-Islami, in its madaris and in families who follow its teaching. Its presentation is more detailed in places, with extra practice exercises and colour-coded pages in many editions, which some children find helpful.

How do they differ?

Both books have the same goal: a student who can read Arabic words correctly and is ready to open the Quran. The differences are in the details, not the destination.

Reach and familiarity

Noorani Qaida is the more widely used of the two, across South Asia and the wider Muslim world. Almost every Quran tutor, whatever their background, has taught from it. Madani Qaida is very common in communities linked to Dawat-e-Islami, and less familiar to tutors outside those circles.

Layout and exercises

Madani Qaida tends to include more practice exercises and more detailed explanations on each page, and many editions use colour to highlight letters and rules. Noorani Qaida is shorter and simpler in layout, which some parents and tutors prefer for young children — there is less on each page to distract them.

Ordering of lessons

The two books introduce some rules in a slightly different order, and give different amounts of space to particular topics. Neither order is right or wrong; they are simply two ways of arranging the same material.

Community connection

For some families, the choice is about community. If your child attends a madrasa or weekend class that uses Madani Qaida, learning the same book at home keeps everything consistent. If your family learned from Noorani Qaida, or your local mosque uses it, the same logic applies in the other direction.

Which one should you choose?

Here is an honest way to decide:

  • Follow your community. If your child already attends a class or madrasa, use the book that class uses. Consistency between home and class matters more than any difference between the books.
  • Follow your family. If you or your parents learned from Noorani Qaida, you will find it easier to help your child practise. The same applies in reverse for families who know Madani Qaida well.
  • Follow your tutor. A tutor teaches best from the book they know thoroughly. Ask the tutor which primer they prefer — and then let them teach it properly.
  • Do not worry about making the "wrong" choice. A child who finishes either Qaida with correct pronunciation is ready for the Quran. The book is a tool; the teaching and the practice are what make the difference.

If your family prefers Madani Qaida, mention it when you book — a tutor can work from either book, and the first trial classes are a good time to discuss it.

"Both primers lead to the same place: a child who can open the Quran and read it. Choose the book your family and your tutor know best — then focus on regular lessons and daily practice."

Can a child switch from one to the other?

Yes, and it is not difficult. Because both books teach the same letters, the same vowel marks and the same basic rules, a student who has worked halfway through one can move to the other without starting again. The tutor simply checks what the student knows, finds the matching point in the new book, and carries on from there.

This is worth knowing if your child starts with one book at a weekend class and then begins online lessons with a tutor who uses the other. It is a small adjustment, not a fresh start.

What matters more than the book?

Three things matter far more than which Qaida you choose:

  • A patient, qualified tutor. One-to-one teaching means every mispronounced letter is heard and corrected on the spot.
  • Regular lessons. Three short lessons a week beat one long lesson. Little and often is how children learn sounds.
  • Short daily practice. A few minutes of reading aloud each day between lessons makes more difference than any textbook.

Get those three things right, and either book will do its job well.

Frequently asked questions

Is Noorani Qaida better than Madani Qaida?

Neither is better as a fact — both are beginner primers that teach the same reading skills. Noorani Qaida is the more widely used standard, while Madani Qaida is popular in communities connected with Dawat-e-Islami and includes more exercises. The best choice is usually the one your community, family or tutor already uses.

Can my child learn from both books at once?

It is possible, but it is usually better to stick to one book so the child follows a single clear order of lessons. If your child attends a class using one book, use the same book for online lessons to keep everything consistent.

Does it matter which Qaida my child finishes before starting the Quran?

No. A child who finishes either Qaida with correct pronunciation is ready to begin Nazra, reading the Quran itself. What matters is that the letters and rules were learned correctly, not which book they were learned from.
