Online Quran Classes Fees — Full Price Guide

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

How much should online Quran classes cost? Search online and you will find prices everywhere from very cheap to surprisingly expensive — and it is hard to tell what you are actually paying for. This guide explains what affects the price, what our own fees are in full, what other academies typically charge, and what to check before you pay anyone.

What affects the price of online Quran classes?

Five things mainly decide what a family pays each month:

1. Days per week

This is the biggest factor. Two classes a week costs less than five, simply because there are fewer lessons. Most academies, including ours, price by the number of days per week. More days means faster progress — but also a higher monthly fee — so the right choice depends on your budget and your goals.

2. Lesson length

Most online Quran lessons are 30 minutes. Longer lessons cost more. Our Hifz program uses 45-minute classes, because memorisation lessons need more time — the student recites new work, recent revision and older revision in every session.

3. One-to-one vs group classes

Group classes are usually cheaper, because one tutor's time is shared between several students. One-to-one classes cost more, but the tutor's full attention is on your child for the whole lesson — every letter heard, every mistake corrected. For Quran learning, where pronunciation is the whole point, that difference is worth understanding before you choose the cheaper option.

4. Hifz vs recitation

Hifz (memorisation) programmes usually cost more than recitation courses. The lessons are longer, the tutor is a certified Hafiz or Hafiza, and the preparation — setting daily Sabaq, Sabqi and Manzil targets and tracking them — takes more of the tutor's time.

5. Tutor qualifications

Certified, Ijazah-holding, background-checked tutors cost an academy more than unqualified ones — and that shows in the fee. A very cheap academy may be cutting corners on exactly the thing that matters most: who is teaching your child.

What do other academies typically charge?

Online Quran academies vary widely, and prices change often — so treat any number as a rough guide rather than a quote. As a general picture, other academies commonly charge around $30 to $85 a month for one-to-one classes, depending on the number of days per week, the lesson length, and the tutor's qualifications.

Our fees sit at the lower end of that range. We can do that because we keep the academy small and simple — no large office, no heavy advertising, no complicated platform of our own. The fee goes to the tutor and to running the academy, not to overhead.

That said, price alone is a poor way to choose. An academy charging $30 a month with unqualified tutors and group classes may be worse value than one charging $50 with certified one-to-one teaching. Always compare what is included, not just the number.

"The cheapest Quran class is not the one with the lowest fee. It is the one where your child actually learns — because a cheap class that teaches nothing is the most expensive lesson of all."

What to check before you pay any academy

  • Is the fee published clearly? If an academy will not state its price plainly on its website, ask why. Hidden fees and "contact us for pricing" are warning signs.
  • Is there a registration fee? Some academies charge an extra one-off fee on top of the monthly price. We do not — and if another academy does, make sure you know the total cost before you commit.
  • Can you cancel anytime? You should never be locked into a long contract for Quran classes. We allow cancellation anytime.
  • Are the classes really one-to-one? Some academies advertise "live classes" that turn out to be group sessions. Ask directly: one student, one tutor?
  • Who is the tutor? Are they certified? Hafiz or Hafiza? Ijazah-certified? Background-checked? Can you request a female tutor? The answers matter more than the price.
  • Is there a free trial? A trustworthy academy lets you try real lessons before you pay. If there is no trial, be cautious. Our first 3 classes are free, with no card required.
  • What happens if you miss a class? Ask about the make-up policy. Life happens — children get sick, families travel — and a fair academy has a clear answer.
  • Are there discounts? Ask about family discounts if more than one child is learning, and about any discount for paying a longer period. We offer a family discount on request.

Free trial advice

Use the free trial properly. It is not just a free sample — it is your chance to check the academy before spending anything. In the 3 free classes, pay attention to:

  • Does the tutor correct your child's mistakes, or just let them read?
  • Does your child enjoy the lesson, or dread it?
  • Does the tutor explain things clearly, in a language your child understands?
  • Do you get a clear sense of what your child will learn next?

If the answer to those questions is yes, the academy is probably worth the fee — whatever the fee is. If the answer is no, no price is low enough.
