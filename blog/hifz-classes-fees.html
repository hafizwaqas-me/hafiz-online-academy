Hifz Classes Fees — What Good Hifz Tuition Costs

November 2026 · Hafiza Online Academy

Hifz is a long commitment — often several years — so the monthly fee matters. Families want to know what Hifz classes should cost, what the fee actually pays for, and how to tell the difference between a fair price and a price that is too good to be true. This guide answers those questions plainly, starting with our own fee, and then explains what to look for when you compare academies.

Prices change over time and vary between academies, so treat any range in this article as general context, not as a quote from another academy. The only exact fee in this article is our own.

What does the fee pay for?

A Hifz fee is not just paying for the minutes on a video call. Good Hifz tuition includes several things that are easy to overlook when you compare prices:

One-to-one teaching

In a one-to-one lesson, the tutor listens to every verse your child recites. In a group class, a child can hide a mispronounced word or a forgotten verse for weeks. One-to-one teaching costs more per student than group teaching, but for memorization — where every mistake matters — it is the format that works.

A qualified, certified tutor

A Hifz tutor needs to know the Quran by heart, to recite with correct Tajweed, and to know how to teach a child — how to set the right lesson size, how to keep revision on track, and how to keep a young student motivated over years. Certified male and female tutors cost more than an untrained teacher, and the difference shows in the quality of the memorization.

Revision, not just new lessons

A proper Hifz lesson is mostly revision. The tutor listens to the Manzil (old memorization), the Sabqi (recent lessons) and then the new Sabaq — and keeps a record of what has been memorized and what needs more work. That record-keeping and planning is part of what you are paying for, and it is what keeps the memorization strong over time.

Flexibility and support

Lesson times that fit your timezone, a choice of platform, a female tutor if that suits your family, and regular progress updates for parents — these are part of the service, and they are worth asking about when you compare academies.

What do other academies charge?

As general context, Hifz plans from other online academies commonly run roughly $55 to $123 per month, depending on how many days a week the student studies, how long the lessons are, and whether the teaching is one-to-one or in a group. That range is broad, and it is approximate — individual academies set their own prices, and prices change — but it gives a sense of the market.

Within that range, the cheapest option is not always the best value, and the most expensive is not always the best teaching. What matters is what the fee includes: one-to-one or group, lesson length, tutor qualifications, and whether revision is a real part of every lesson or an afterthought. A lower fee for group classes with short lessons may work out as worse value than a higher fee for one-to-one lessons with proper revision.

Our Hifz fee of $65 per month sits towards the lower end of that general range, for 5 days a week of 45-minute one-to-one lessons. We keep it there deliberately: our aim is to make Hifz affordable for families worldwide, while paying certified tutors fairly for the skilled work they do.

Hifz costs a little more than our other 5-day plan ($55) because the lessons are longer — 45 minutes instead of 30 — and a Hifz lesson is more demanding for the tutor, with a full Manzil, Sabqi and Sabaq revision cycle to listen to and track.

Questions to ask before you enrol

Before you choose an academy on price alone, ask these questions. The answers will tell you more about value than the monthly fee will:

  • Is the teaching one-to-one or in a group? For Hifz, one-to-one is strongly recommended.
  • How long is each lesson? A 45-minute lesson allows time for proper revision. A very short lesson may not.
  • How many days a week? Five days a week is the usual recommendation for Hifz, because daily contact keeps memorization and revision fresh.
  • Are the tutors certified? Ask whether tutors are certified and whether female tutors are available for kids and sisters.
  • How is revision handled? Ask whether every lesson includes Sabaq, Sabqi and Manzil, and how the tutor tracks what has been memorized.
  • Is there a free trial? A trial lets you meet the tutor and see a real lesson before you pay. We offer 3 free classes with no card required.
  • Are there extra fees? Ask about registration fees, and whether you can cancel anytime. We charge no registration fee, and you can cancel anytime.
  • Is there a family discount? If more than one child in your family is learning, ask. We offer a family discount on request.
